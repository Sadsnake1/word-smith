# The scratch pad

[← Back to the README](../README.md)

A note of its own beside every note, in the right sidebar, the way Scrivener keeps a document's notes beside it. Write down what the scene needs, a name you mean to look up, the thing you'll fix tomorrow: it stays with that scene, and the next scene has its own.

## Opening it

Run **Open the scratch pad** from the command palette, or press **Open** under **Settings → Manuscript → Scratch pad**. The pad of the note you're in opens in the right sidebar with the caret in it. **Show or hide the scratch pad** opens or closes the pane without moving the caret; both can take a hotkey.

While the pane is open it follows you. Open Chapter 3, Scene 2, or click into a pane that holds it, and its pad takes the sidebar's place; go back to Chapter 1, Scene 1 and its pad is there again, as you left it. Clicking into the pad itself, or into a canvas or the Organizer, leaves the pad as it is. Close the pane and nothing follows until you open it again.

## Where the pads are kept

Each pad is an ordinary note, at its note's own path under **Word-Smith/Scratch pads**: `Book/Chapter 1/Scene 1.md` keeps its pad at `Word-Smith/Scratch pads/Book/Chapter 1/Scene 1.md`. The pad's tab carries the scene's name, and links, lists and formatting work as in any note.

A note gets its pad, empty, the first time you open it with the pane open. Rename or move a note, or the folder it is in, and its pad goes with it. Delete a note and its pad stays, in case you still want what you wrote there.

Pads are notes about the writing, never the writing: they count toward no word count, target, daily target or history, they are not rows in the Organizer, and they are never compiled into the book. **Hide the Word-Smith folder** (Settings → Vault) hides them with the rest of the plugin's files.

**Scratch pad folder** under **Settings → Manuscript → Scratch pad** moves where new pads are made. Pads already made stay where they are; move the folder yourself to bring them along.
